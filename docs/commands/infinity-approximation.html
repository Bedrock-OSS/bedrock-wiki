Introduction

Almost everything in CCT is based on construct a limit expression, obtain target outcomes by means of approximation. We call it Limit Approximation, It is the most important part of CCT. It is a tool by using very extreme value (close to infinity or zero) to make the formula in CCT be available.

Minecraft makes the coordinates parameters (x, y, z, rx and ry) as a floating point number. It will automatically round decimals with a large digits. To achieve this effect, you need to gives the limit parameters a very extreme value.

In different limit expression, the fittable least values you assign is different, it's up to the convergence rate of the expression, error magnitude in game and required precision you want.

facing Gives a Direction

First, understand exactly what facing does.
Let the current position be PPP, and the target be QQQ. After executing facing, the view direction toward QQQ. Then move d blocks with:

positioned ^ ^ ^d

The key point: the distance is precisely d.
No matter how far apart PPP and QQQ are, the direction produced by facing always has a length of 1. It normalizes the displacement "from PPP to QQQ" into a unit‑length vector.
This means facing gives you a direction for free, and this direction encodes the proportional positional relationship between PPP and QQQ. The infinity approximation to extract this proportional information.
The extraction method is to construct a right‑angled triangle and apply similar‑triangle geometry.

You end up at a point starting from PPP, along the direction toward QQQ, at an exact distance of d blocks.

Find the Midpoint by Similar-triangles

Let SSS and MMM be two points at the same elevation with a horizontal separation DDD. We wish to find their midpoint.

Step 1
Raise the origin far above SSS to a height denoted HHH; call this new position S′S'S′.

Step 2
Look from S′S'S′ toward MMM. This line of sight forms the hypotenuse (named its length as LLL) of the right triangle S′SMS'SMS′SM. By the Pythagorean theorem:

L=H2+D2L = \sqrt{H^2 + D^2}L=H2+D2​

Step 3
Travel H2\frac{H}{2}2H​ blocks along this hypotenuse.

The traversed segment shares the same direction as the full hypotenuse, so the resulting smaller triangle is similar to S′SMS'SMS′SM, with similarity ratio:

H2L\frac{\frac{H}{2}}{L}L2H​​

The actual horizontal displacement is therefore:

H2L×D=HD2L\frac{\frac{H}{2}}{L}\times D = \frac{HD}{2L}L2H​​×D=2LHD​

The actual vertical drop is:

H2L×H=H22L\frac{\frac{H}{2}}{L}\times H = \frac{H^2}{2L}L2H​​×H=2LH2​

Step 4
We are still not back to the original elevation, so compensate downward using relative coordinates.

Now for the crucial observation. When HHH is much larger than DDD:

L=H2+D2=H1+D2H2≈HL = \sqrt{H^2+D^2} = H\sqrt{1+\frac{D^2}{H^2}} \approx HL=H2+D2​=H1+H2D2​​≈H

since D2H2\frac{D^2}{H^2}H2D2​ is a vanishingly small quantity. Substitute back into the horizontal displacement:

HD2L≈HD2H=D2\frac{HD}{2L} \approx \frac{HD}{2H} = \frac{D}{2}2LHD​≈2HHD​=2D​

This is exactly half the horizontal distance. The midpoint is recovered.

Meanwhile the vertical drop approximates to H2\frac{H}{2}2H​, so step 4 requires an additional downward offset of H2\frac{H}{2}2H​ to return to the starting elevation.

Generalise to Arbitrary Ratios
Replace H2\frac{H}{2}2H​ with tHtHtH. The horizontal displacement becomes:

tHDL≈tD\frac{tHD}{L} \approx tDLtHD​≈tD

The vertical drop is approximately tHtHtH, descending from height HHH to roughly (1−t)H(1-t)H(1−t)H. Step 4 therefore needs a further downward offset of (1−t)H(1-t)H(1−t)H.

This constitutes the complete formula for proportional‑point computation.

Looking back at the process: we take a fixed length HHH as the "radius", and locate new points by rotating direction and marking off lengths. This follows the same logic as compass construction — in compass-and-straightedge geometry operations such as finding midpoints and drawing perpendicular lines essentially also rely on circles of fixed radius to locate intersection points.

We save the data in the "radius" and the "angle" of this "compass" The difference us that the radius of a traditional compass is comparable to the scale of the figure, while HHH here must be far larger than the figure scale. The approximation L≈HL \approx HL≈H is only valid under this premise. This is where the term large number compass comes from.

Let us quantify the error. The exact value of the horizontal displacement is:
tHDH2+D2\frac{tHD}{\sqrt{H^2+D^2}}H2+D2​tHD​

How Large Should HHH Be

Judging from the formula, a larger HHH is always preferable, but it cannot be increased infinitely in practice.

Game coordinates are stored as floating-point numbers with limited precision. A core property of floating-point numbers is fixed relative precision: the greater the magnitude of a value, the larger the minimum interval it can represent. When HHH reaches the order of hundreds of thousands, while the graphics we work with are only a few blocks in size, the lower-order bits of the small coordinate values will be lost during calculations, overwhelmed by the magnitude of the large coordinate.

Expressed as a model, the total error is the sum of two terms:
E(H)≈C1r2H+C2εHE(H) \approx C_1\frac{r^2}{H} + C_2\varepsilon HE(H)≈C1​Hr2​+C2​εH

The first is the geometric truncation error, which decreases as HHH grows larger. The second terms is the floating-point error, which increases as HHH grows larger. Here, rrr denotes the typical scale of the figure, and εεε is the relative precision of floating-point numbers.

The total error reaches its minimum when the two terms are equal:
H∼rεH \sim \frac{r}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}H∼ε​r​

For single-precision floating-point numbers, ε≈2−23\varepsilon \approx 2^{-23}ε≈2−23. Substituting this gives 1ε≈2900\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}} \approx 2900ε​1​≈2900, or:
H∼2900 rH \sim 2900\,rH∼2900r

This explains why values such as 102410241024, 204820482048, 409640964096 and 163841638416384 are commonly seen in community formulas — they all fall within the reasonable range for figures with a scale of several to several dozen blocks

This estimation only gives the order of magnitude. The proper approach is to test multiple values of HHH for comparison:

256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192

Note when modifying HHH: all related distances in the formula must be updated simultaneously. The midpoint formula involves HHH, tHtHtH and (1−t)H(1-t)H(1−t)H — changing only one of them will directly cause errors. The far-point reflection below uses HHH, 2H2H2H and HHH, and the same rule applies.

Summary

The entire validity of the infinity approximation rests on a single approximation:
H2+D2≈H(H≫D)\sqrt{H^2+D^2} \approx H \qquad (H \gg D)H2+D2​≈H(H≫D)

Two essential facts follow from this approximation:

  • The infinity approximation relies on asymptotic approximation, not an exact identity.
  • There exists an optimal range for HHH; a larger value is not always better.

Understanding this is critical. The error analysis of all advanced formulas in subsequent chapters is simply a variation and extension of this principle. When a approximated figure shows slight deviation, jagged edges or double lines with the precise figure, the first thing to verify is whether the relationship between HHH and the figure scale is appropriate.
